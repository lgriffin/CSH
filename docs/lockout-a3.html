1Background why this matters

A sign-in service must stop password guessing without locking out the real account holder. Three teams of people describe that behaviour, each with a practice that promises alignment. EARS promises one unambiguous way to write a requirement. BDD promises one shared example that business and engineering agree on. TDD promises that design and verification move together. Each unifies inside its own boundary. None of them is judged against the others.

4Root cause analysis each question as deep as the evidence goes

  1. Q1

    Why can all three be green while they disagree?

    Each is judged by its own oracle. Tests pass against the code they drove. Scenarios pass against their step definitions. Sentences pass review one at a time. No oracle reads another practice's artefact.

    4 of 4 tests pass and csh gate allows, while csh check reports 15 signals.

    Evidence stops: this is the condition, the questions below explain it.
  2. Q2

    Why do they disagree on the third attempt?

    All three translated "three failed attempts" from the story independently. Ordinals in prose read two ways: the third one locks, or three are allowed.

    src/lockout.ts: failedAttempts > MAX_FAILED_ATTEMPTS; finding 776bf7df8a4c2145.

    5 Whys to the root, below.
  3. Q3

    Why does EARS contradict itself?

    Each sentence was written and reviewed alone, and EARS has no rule for how two sentences combine. The WHILE clause that would separate them was left out of LCK-003.

    joint-conflict 6aa446d724b76ed9: locked, correct password, no valid outcome.

    Evidence stops: reading 2, a missing context. Only the owner can confirm it.
  4. Q4

    Why does meaning drift between notations?

    Every hand-off is a translation by a person or a table: WHILE became and, minutes became seconds, ticket identifiers were renumbered. Bindings and step definitions are code that nobody reviews as requirements.

    shape-mismatch on LCK-002; unit-mismatch on the fifteen-minute scenario; dangling-citation @LOCK-3.

    Evidence stops: the translations exist only as code.
  5. Q5

    Why are some cases nobody's?

    Each practice can express things the others cannot. EARS can say WHERE and talk about email; BDD can describe a support workflow; TDD can pin down the fourth attempt. What one practice says alone has nothing to agree or disagree with.

    uncited LCK-004 and LCK-005; unliftable support scenario; no-rule on the fourth-attempt test.

    Evidence stops: these are scope decisions for the owner.
  6. Q6

    Why would a BDD and TDD contradiction go unseen even with CSH?

    Q-EX compares an example only with rules, never with another example. The scenario and the test on the third attempt collide only because LCK-001 has a predicate. Delete that rule and the contradiction is silent.

    packages/check/src/run.ts, the Q-EX loop.

    Ends in a tool: countermeasure C7.

4The 5 Whys for the most harmful signal, the off-by-one, asked until the answer is a process or a tool

  1. Why 1

    Why does the shipped code lock on the fourth failure?

    It was written to pass allows three failed attempts before locking, the first test written (test/lockout.test.ts).

  2. Why 2

    Why did the test allow three?

    The developer read "three failed attempts" in the story as a limit (MAX_FAILED_ATTEMPTS = 3 with >). The test came from the story, not from LCK-001 or the agreed scenario.

  3. Why 3

    Why from the story and not from the agreed artefacts?

    A unit test has no way to cite a requirement or a scenario. The witness format carries no citation (Evidence tab, section 2), so nothing links the test to LCK-001 and nothing prompts the developer to look.

  4. Why 4

    Why did the scenario, which agrees with LCK-001, not catch it?

    The scenario and the test are both examples, and nothing compares examples with each other: not the BDD runner, not the test runner, and not Q-EX in CSH. The contradiction surfaces only because LCK-001 has a predicate.

  5. Why 5

    Why does it take a rule for two practices to meet?

    The three practices share no artefact between the story and the code. Each translates the story on its own, so they are parallel lanes, not a chain, and they meet only in the running system.

    Root cause, in a process: no shared hub. Countermeasures C1 (process) and C7, C8 (harness).
